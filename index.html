Unwrap

The app lives in the menu bar (¶ icon). Select text anywhere, then press a shortcut or right-click the selection (in some apps the items are under Services):

⇧⌘C Copy without Line Breaks – the cleaned text is put on the clipboard, ready to paste.
⇧⌘X Translate with DeepL without Line Breaks – the cleaned text is also opened in the DeepL app, or in DeepL's web translator if the app is not installed.

The text is copied without styles, line breaks and end-of-line hyphens. A normal ⌘C keeps working as usual.

In apps that have their own ⇧⌘C or ⇧⌘X, that one wins; use the right-click menu there. You can change both shortcuts in System Settings › Keyboard › Keyboard Shortcuts › Services › Text. DeepL removes line breaks from text it receives, so to keep paragraphs Unwrap pastes the text into DeepL again; macOS asks you once to allow Unwrap under Privacy & Security › Accessibility for that.

The TC39 committee is comprised of between 50 and about
100 different people from a broad section of web-invested
companies, such as browser makers (Mozilla, Google, Apple)
and device makers (Samsung, etc). All members of the commit-
tee are volunteers, though many of them are employees of
these companies and so may receive compensation in part for
their duties on the committee.

TC39 meets generally about every other month, usually for
about three days, to review work done by members since the
last meeting, discuss issues, and vote on proposals. Meeting
locations rotate among member companies willing to host.
All TC39 proposals progress through a five-stage process—
of course, since we’re programmers, it’s 0-based!—Stage 0
through Stage 4.

Menu bar options

  • Remove All Line Breaks – everything becomes one line.
  • Keep Paragraphs and Headings – wrapped lines are joined, every heading and paragraph stays on its own line, with an empty line after each paragraph.
  • Only Remove Styles – plain text, line breaks untouched.
  • Also Clean Every ⌘C Copy – the old behaviour: every copy is cleaned automatically.
